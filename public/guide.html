A Visitor's Guide to the Timewave

for readers who prefer paper and patience

The instrument itself lives at timewave-zero-ui.vercel.app. This page reads well on paper — printing it makes a small pamphlet you can keep beside the screen.

What this is

In the 1970s, Terence McKenna proposed that time itself has a shape — that history doesn't just happen but rises and falls, like weather, between habit and novelty. He drew that shape out of the I Ching, the ancient Chinese Book of Changes: from the traditional ordering of its 64 hexagrams he derived a long, jagged line, repeated it inside itself at every scale of time, and declared that the whole pattern ran downhill toward a final day — December 21, 2012 — when novelty would become infinite and history would end. He called it Timewave Zero. From 1974 until his death in 2000 it existed as actual computer software, and he spent twenty years walking audiences along the wave, pointing at its dips and peaks.

That day came and went quietly, and the world kept on. This project restored the timewave anyway — the way a museum restores an orrery or an astrolabe: a beautiful, wrong model of the cosmos, in full working order. The mathematics is the original mathematics, checked digit for digit against the software McKenna actually used. The claims are labeled honestly. And the instrument is yours to wander.

What you're looking at

One line, drawn in amber: the wave. Time flows left to right, and the line falls toward its zero at the right edge — a soft glow marks the terminus, December 21, 2012. When the line is high, McKenna said, the world is in the grip of habit — settled, repetitive, conservative. When it plunges, novelty is pouring in: invention, upheaval, connection, change. Small dots along the wave mark historical events, from the Big Bang to the moon landing. The violet diamonds are McKenna's own story — the Amazon experiment that started it all, his books, his software, the mathematician who found the flaw, his death in 2000 — his life, plotted on the very curve he believed explained it.

How to move

There is really only one trick: drag the picture to slide through time, and scroll (or pinch) to zoom. Everything else is a convenience. The row of buttons along the top — All time, History, 67 years, Final year, Terminus, The void — are prepared viewpoints; if you ever feel lost, press History and you are home. Resting your cursor anywhere on the wave opens a small card that tells you the date, the wave's value, and which hexagram of the I Ching that day belongs to.

A suggested walk

  1. Press “History.” Four thousand years in one view: the Bronze Age high, the long slide through antiquity, the medieval trough, and at the far right the modern plunge to zero. This is the picture McKenna showed most often.
  2. Find the violet diamonds. Hover the ones crowded near the right edge: La Chorrera 1971, the software in 1987, the objection in 1996, his death in 2000. The theory's own history rides the theory's curve.
  3. Press “67 years.” The final cycle, 1945 to 2012 — Hiroshima at the left edge, the postwar climb, a peak at the Summer of Love, then the long descent through the rest of the century. McKenna read this screen aloud for twenty years.
  4. Press the ⇡64 button, and look closely. You have leapt to a span 64 times longer — four thousand years instead of sixty-seven — and yet the curve is exactly the same shape. The timewave repeats inside itself, exactly, at every scale. Unlike the rest of the theory, this claim is simply true. McKenna called it resonance; the sixties, he liked to say, were the fall of Rome played 64 times faster.
  5. Press “Terminus.” The last days before zero. The strip along the bottom names each six-day span after a hexagram; the sequence ends, by the old ordering's own logic, on hexagram 64 — ䷿ Before Completion. The wave wanders down and touches zero exactly at 6 a.m. on December 21, 2012, Amazon time.
  6. Press “The void.” Everything after that morning. The wave simply ends; the years since float as pale ghosts in the dark — the pandemics and telescopes and machines that arrived after the end of history. A small white dot marks you are here.
  7. Try the five names in the top corner. Kelley, Watkins, Sheliak, Huang Ti — four historical versions of the wave's numbers, from the 1975 original to a 1998 correction — and, in purple, the Seed: the raw King Wen wave they all grew from, before any of the disputed steps. Each redraws the curve, and a card at the top right says what that version did to the seed. Notice that each was, in its day, said to fit history well — a quiet lesson in how easily a wandering line finds meaning.

What is true here, and what isn't

The mathematics is real: the wave genuinely comes from the King Wen sequence of the I Ching by fixed rules, and its self-similarity is exact — we prove both, every time the software is tested, against McKenna's original program. The I Ching's ordering really does hold the curious patterns McKenna noticed; some of his statistical claims about it even check out. What was never real is the reading: "novelty" was never measured, the end date was chosen rather than derived — and moved, once, to match the Maya calendar — and December 21, 2012 passed like any other Friday. The restoration keeps both truths in view, the way a good museum does: the craft stays visible, and the claims stay accurate.

A note on spirit: nothing here mocks him. McKenna knew the risk he was taking — a theory with a date can fail in public, and his did, twelve years after his death. It is precisely because he wagered so openly that the instrument deserves to run again.

A few words, plainly

Hexagram ䷊
A stack of six lines, broken or solid — the I Ching's 64 symbols. In the timewave's calendar, each hexagram governs six days.
King Wen sequence
The traditional order of the 64 hexagrams, some three thousand years old. The number of lines that change between each hexagram and the next is the seed of the entire wave.
Novelty
McKenna's word for newness itself — connection, complexity, surprise. Downhill on the graph means more of it.
The zero date
Where the wave runs out: December 21, 2012. The wave touches zero there once, and is silent afterward.
