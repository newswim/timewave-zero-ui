Timewave Zero, restored

In the 1970s Terence McKenna proposed that time has a shape: a wave of rising and falling "novelty," derived from the 64 hexagrams of the I Ching, repeating at every scale, and ending on December 21, 2012. He ran it as computer software for twenty years. This site restores that software's mathematics and checks it, on every change, against the original program.

When the line is high, the world runs on habit; when it falls, novelty pours in. Press ⇡64 and the view widens 64 times while the curve keeps its exact shape. That self-similarity is the one part of the theory that is simply true. At close zoom the final cycle resolves into the 64 hexagrams, six days each, ending on Before Completion.

"Novelty" was never given a measurable definition. The end date was chosen, then moved to match the Maya calendar. The day itself passed without incident. We restored the instrument anyway, the way a museum restores an orrery, and labeled it accurately. The story, the mathematics, and the sources are on the About page; a printable introduction is in the Visitor's Guide.
